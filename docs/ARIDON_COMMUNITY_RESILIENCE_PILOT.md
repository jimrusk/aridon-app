# Aridon Community Resilience Pilot

## Mission
Prove that one community can become cheaper to live in, safer to operate, more resilient to water and communications failures, and easier for small businesses to survive by coordinating six workstreams through one measurable operating layer.

## First demonstration
- 100 households
- 25 small businesses
- 1 public or cooperative water system
- 1 rural emergency communications zone
- 1 city, county, Tribe, cooperative, utility, housing authority, or nonprofit lead partner

## Six workstreams

### 1. Cost of living
Interventions may include plug-in solar where legal, weatherization, energy-efficiency measures, bill optimization, local food access, fraud-loss prevention, and public-benefit navigation.

Primary metrics: monthly household savings, kWh reduced, energy burden, avoided fraud losses, enrollment and retention.

### 2. Housing
Build a pipeline around vacant-property discovery, rehabilitation, infill, ADUs, modular/panelized housing, financing, contractor coordination and occupancy.

Primary metrics: units identified, units recovered, cost per unit, time to occupancy, financing secured and monthly housing-cost reduction.

### 3. Small business
Deploy Aridon Business OS as a virtual back office for cash flow, receivables, sales follow-up, marketing, procurement, scheduling, funding discovery, bookkeeping organization and government-contract readiness.

Primary metrics: owner hours saved, receivables accelerated, operating-cost reduction, qualified leads, capital secured, revenue retained and business-survival indicators.

### 4. Trust Layer + Fraud Shield
Screen suspicious messages, links, invoices, vendor-bank changes, payment instructions and high-impact actions. Produce an explainable decision and a Trust Receipt before consequential activity proceeds.

Primary metrics: checks completed, high-risk actions held, confirmed fraud prevented, false-positive rate, verification completion time and dollars protected.

### 5. Water resilience
Combine forecasting, conservation, leak detection, reuse, treatment, supplemental supply, emergency reserve and cybersecurity into a single water-operations view. AWG should be positioned as a supplemental and emergency resource, not the only answer.

Primary metrics: gallons conserved, non-revenue water reduced, reuse/treatment output, supplemental supply, outage minutes, reserve days and response time.

### 6. Rural emergency communications
Use layered communications rather than a single carrier: existing fiber/cellular/public-safety radio first, satellite backup, low-bandwidth mesh/LoRa for alerts and sensors where appropriate, and solar/battery power at critical nodes.

Primary metrics: coverage added, critical nodes protected, failover time, backup runtime, alert-delivery success and outage recovery time.

## 90-day pilot structure

### Days 0-30: Baseline + enrollment
- Confirm public lead partner and operating area.
- Enroll 100 households and 25 businesses.
- Establish baseline bills, costs, fraud exposure, business operating metrics, water losses and communications coverage.
- Inventory housing opportunities and public/private funding paths.
- Select critical communications nodes.
- Establish data-sharing, privacy, consent and reporting rules.

### Days 31-60: Interventions
- Launch first household cost interventions.
- Deploy Business OS with participating businesses.
- Run Trust Layer/Fraud Shield for enrolled participants and partner staff.
- Begin housing rehabilitation/finance pipeline.
- Implement water monitoring/operations improvements.
- Install or configure emergency communications backup where approved.

### Days 61-90: Measure + scale decision
- Compare baseline and post-intervention metrics.
- Validate savings and operational outcomes.
- Produce a government-grade impact report.
- Identify interventions that should stop, continue or scale.
- Build a second-phase funding and procurement package.

## Government-facing offer
Aridon should not ask a government to fund 'AI'. The offer is:

> Give us one community and a defined 90-day pilot. We will measure whether the program can lower household costs, strengthen small businesses, protect residents from fraud, improve housing utilization, increase water resilience and keep critical communications operating during outages.

Aridon can serve as technology integrator and measurement layer while eligible public, nonprofit, utility, cooperative or Tribal partners remain lead applicants where funding rules require it.

## Partner stack
- City/county/Tribal government: convening, permissions, public-benefit alignment.
- Housing authority/nonprofit: rehabilitation and housing finance.
- Utility/co-op: energy data, efficiency, distributed energy coordination.
- SBDC/chamber/community college: small-business recruitment and support.
- Water utility/system: water baseline, operations and resilience.
- Emergency management/fire/EMS: communications requirements and exercises.
- Banks/credit unions: fraud shield distribution and small-business finance.
- Insurers: loss-prevention validation and possible premium partnerships.

## Funding lanes to pursue
Use program-fit screening before applying. Likely lanes include federal/state/local housing funds, rural-development programs, water infrastructure and technical-assistance programs, energy-efficiency/weatherization programs, rural broadband/telecommunications programs, cybersecurity/resilience grants, small-business technical assistance and philanthropic community-resilience capital.

## Scale gate
Do not scale a workstream merely because it sounds good. Scale only when baseline-to-after evidence shows measurable public value, acceptable cost per outcome, manageable implementation risk and a repeatable delivery model.
