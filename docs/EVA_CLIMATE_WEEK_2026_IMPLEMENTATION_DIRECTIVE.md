# Eva Climate Week NYC 2026 Implementation Directive

## Objective
Eva owns the conversion of Food Tank / Climate Week NYC 2026 findings into Aridon implementation work. Do not treat this as a conference-summary archive. Convert validated ideas into pilots, partnerships, product requirements, funding opportunities, buyer relationships, and measurable demonstrations.

## Source series
Food Tank Climate Week NYC 2026, Sept. 20-25, including Putting Farmers First, Solving Food Waste, Mission Driven Brands Sustainability Summit, People-Focused Climate-Friendly Food-Forward NYC, Thriving Farms and Ranches, Nourishing the Future, Roots of Organic: From Soil to Shelf, Rapid Response / Food Security, Chefs Save the World, Water and Blue Foods / Planet Aqua, Food Education Summit, Food & Agriculture Film Festival, and additional Food Tank sessions and follow-up materials.

## Verified themes to carry forward
1. Producers cannot be expected to finance food-system transition alone.
2. Producer profitability and durable market access must be designed into transition programs.
3. Water is food-system infrastructure and should be assessed as physical, operational and financial risk.
4. Early warning has value only when it connects to permission, financing, equipment, people and implementation.
5. Buyers, chefs, schools, institutions, processors and brands can create demand signals before production.
6. Traceability and evidence matter to buyers and credible claims.
7. Young producers need education plus land/resource access, water, energy, equipment, capital and markets.
8. Food waste requires actual collection/processing/end-market infrastructure.
9. Farming practices, nutrition and food quality should be measurable where evidence permits.
10. Storytelling should be built from verified Farm Passport evidence rather than unsupported claims.
11. Compound risks must be modeled together because drought, heat, wildfire, pests, water shortage, feed/input prices, buyer concentration, logistics and other shocks can amplify one another.
12. Circular infrastructure should begin modularly, prove regional economics and feedstock/end-market fit, then scale.

## Eva implementation architecture
Producer -> Eva assessment -> Producer Profitability Engine -> constraint/risk -> intervention portfolio -> Capital Fit -> Water/Energy/Equipment -> implementation team -> Buyer Match -> Processing & Logistics -> Farm Passport evidence -> buyer/customer -> measured producer return -> learning loop.

## Eva Intervention Engine
Data -> detect -> verify confidence -> economic exposure -> intervention options -> funding -> permissions -> equipment/resources -> implementation people -> buyer consequences -> human approval for consequential actions -> execute/coordinate -> measure.

Every intervention must answer: Is funding ready? Is permission/authority ready? Is equipment/infrastructure ready? Is an implementation team ready? Is a buyer/market ready? What is the expected producer economic effect?

## Compound Resilience Engine
Eva and Farm OS should evaluate interacting risks rather than isolated alerts.

Potential signals include:
- drought and precipitation outlook
- well, storage, stream or irrigation-water availability
- extreme heat/cold and wildfire/smoke exposure
- pest/disease pressure where reliable data exists
- soil/crop/livestock condition
- feed, fertilizer, fuel and other input prices
- energy availability/cost
- buyer concentration, contract loss or price deterioration
- processing/cold-storage capacity
- transportation/logistics disruption
- labor/equipment constraints
- financing/liquidity constraints

Operating flow:
Signals -> individual risk estimates -> interaction/compound-risk assessment -> confidence -> expected production effect -> dollar exposure -> time-to-impact -> ranked intervention portfolio -> funding/permission/equipment/people/buyer gates -> human approval when consequential -> implementation -> measured avoided loss / realized outcome.

Do not simply add risk scores together. Where evidence is insufficient to estimate interactions, label uncertainty and present scenarios instead of false precision.

Example intervention portfolio may include water storage or AWG where technically/economically appropriate, irrigation changes, agrivoltaics/energy measures, crop/feed alternatives, Capital Fit, Surplus Prevention, alternate Buyer Match customers, processing/storage/logistics, insurance/risk-management options and implementation support.

Primary outputs should include dollars of producer exposure, expected cost of intervention, expected loss avoided, resilience effect and confidence/range.

## Water Resilience
Water Resilience Score -> production/economic exposure -> portfolio of solutions -> capital requirement -> financing -> implementation -> gallons and production outcomes. AWG 30K is one tool, not the answer to every water problem.

## Farm Passport + Buyer Match
Capture verified production, provenance, water, energy, processing, certifications/practices and outcome evidence. Connect producer supply to retailer, processor, chef, restaurant, school, institutional and foodservice demand. Avoid unsupported environmental/health claims.

## Young Producer Launchpad
Food education -> hands-on farm experience -> Farm OS -> business/profitability plan -> resource assessment -> Capital Fit -> shared equipment -> water/energy -> Buyer Match -> operating producer.

## Chef / Institutional Buyer layer
Allow buyers to publish product, quantity, specification, delivery window, geography and other procurement needs so Eva can expose demand before production decisions.

## Farm Story
Generate optional producer/buyer storytelling only from verified Farm Passport facts.

## Modular Circular Infrastructure
Build circular food-system capability in stages rather than assuming large centralized infrastructure is automatically economical.

Pilot flow:
Regional farm/processor/restaurant/grocery/institution streams -> classify edible/safe/legal destination -> Surplus & Waste Prevention Engine -> alternate human-food market/recovery -> lawful Circular Feed pathway -> nutrient/compost/energy recovery -> residual disposal.

For each module Eva should measure:
- feedstock/source volume and seasonality
- contamination/quality
- collection/transport cost
- processing/storage cost
- recovered food value
- feed replacement value where applicable
- compost/nutrient value
- energy consumption/production where applicable
- producer/customer savings
- revenue potential
- residual waste
- net unit economics

Scale only after evidence demonstrates sufficient feedstock, reliable end markets, regulatory/safety feasibility and positive strategic/economic value.

## Dryland / Arid Agriculture Test Track
Use STC and participating Southwest farms to compare appropriate dryland/arid-region crops and seed varieties, soil practices, water demand, agrivoltaics, irrigation approaches and economics under real conditions. Combine producer/local knowledge with technology rather than treating technology as a replacement for producer expertise.

Measure yield, water intensity, energy, input cost, resilience, buyer demand and net producer return. Do not make seed/crop recommendations without agronomic and market evidence.

## Southwest Technology Campus pilots
1. Water Resilience Demonstration Farm: compare AWG where appropriate, storage, irrigation efficiency, agrivoltaics and other sources; measure energy, gallons, crop/livestock outcomes, reliability and economics.
2. Eva Early-Warning + Compound Resilience Farm: connect farm/weather/water/market/input/logistics signals to actual interventions and measure avoided losses/outcomes.
3. Farm Passport to Buyer demonstration: farm -> processing -> buyer traceability/evidence.
4. Young Producer Launchpad: education through viable farm/ranch business.
5. Chef + Buyer Demonstration Kitchen: producers/processors/chefs/institutional buyers test products and convert demand into production planning.
6. Modular Food Recovery & Circular Systems pilot sized to actual regional feedstocks and end markets.
7. Water + Food + Energy resilience demonstration center for replicable Southwest deployment.
8. Dryland / Arid Agriculture Test Track.

## Operating rules
- Separate verified speaker/source statements from Aridon analysis.
- Never imply a speaker/organization endorses Aridon, AWG or STC unless confirmed.
- Prefer measurable pilot proposals over generic partnership requests.
- Producer profitability first.
- Do not recommend technology without cost, benefit, funding path, implementation requirements and resilience effect.
- Maintain a cumulative Climate Week implementation ledger: insight, source, Aridon module, partner, pilot, funding path, buyer opportunity, owner, next action, status, evidence/outcome.
- Surface duplicate initiatives and combine them.
- Rank execution internally by feasibility, producer value, partner leverage, funding readiness and ability to demonstrate at STC.
