## 1. Executive proposal

Aridon proposes a **12-month New Mexico agricultural R&D demonstration** designed to convert promising agricultural technology into independently measured field evidence.

The program combines two proof layers:

1. **A 90-day producer learning cohort of 25–50 New Mexico farms and ranches** using Aridon Ag to test business, water-resilience and decision-support usefulness.
2. **A minimum 180-day instrumented field demonstration** testing a controlled water-security stack that may include RootIQ Precision Water, the Aridon Water Independence System, an AWG-1000 research prototype, water storage, telemetry and selected early-warning or livestock modules.

The proposed program is intentionally field-first. Hardware and agronomic performance remain design hypotheses until engineering and independent field validation support them.

**Target program capitalization: $700,000.**

The immediate request is not for FSLC or Food Tank to accept performance claims or act as direct funders. The request is to explore formation of a credible demonstration consortium that can align policy, farmers, researchers, engineering partners and public/private R&D capital.

## 2. Why this program fits FSLC priorities

The Food Security Leadership Council has called for a major renewal of U.S. agricultural R&D investment focused on sustainable productivity and profitability. Its broader food-security framework emphasizes productive crops and livestock, healthy soil, well-managed water, functioning markets and diversified food systems, supported by pathways that include food and agricultural innovation and public data / early warning.

The Aridon demonstration is built around those same operating problems:

- **Well-managed water:** supplemental atmospheric water research, water-source orchestration, storage and precision irrigation.
- **Productive crops and livestock:** root-zone sensing, livestock water/heat monitoring and farmer economics.
- **Healthy soil:** irrigation timing, moisture / tension measurement and Soil Water Battery research.
- **Functioning markets:** Aridon Ag buyer, margin and farm-profitability workflows.
- **Public data and early warning:** permissioned field observations combined with public weather and risk data.
- **Agricultural innovation:** a common evidence architecture that helps move technology from prototype to repeatable field validation.

The demonstration is also designed to answer the practical question behind national R&D policy: **which technologies actually improve farmer outcomes enough to deserve larger public and private investment?**

## 3. Why Food Tank is a strong convening fit

Food Tank has repeatedly emphasized that technology should solve real producer problems and that innovation spreads through relationships, farmer knowledge and trusted networks.

The proposed pilot incorporates that principle directly:

- Farmers participate in problem definition before technology is deployed.
- Producer time for structured co-design and feedback is budgeted.
- Private operating data remain permissioned.
- Measured results and modeled scenarios are visibly separated.
- Public storytelling occurs only after evidence review and with agreed permissions.
- Independent researchers are invited to challenge the technology, not merely validate a sales narrative.

A Food Tank role could include convening, farmer voice, introductions to researchers/funders, and a transparent public-learning event or editorial pathway if Food Tank independently determines the results warrant it.

No coverage, endorsement or event commitment is assumed.

## 4. Proposed New Mexico demonstration

### Layer 1: 90-day producer learning cohort

**Participants:** 25–50 volunteer New Mexico producers.

Potential mix:

- crop operations;
- cow-calf / livestock operations;
- irrigated agriculture;
- specialty crops;
- diversified farms and ranches.

Aridon provides onboarding, training, office hours and the Aridon Ag operating layer.

Measured outcomes may include:

- producer activation and engagement;
- administrative/reporting time saved;
- sales opportunities surfaced;
- labor or overtime opportunities identified;
- input / inventory visibility;
- crop or field profitability insights;
- water-resilience planning usefulness;
- producer satisfaction and willingness to continue.

### Layer 2: 180-day minimum instrumented field demonstration

A primary field site should allow a controlled comparison using comparable soils, crop class and operating conditions where practical.

**Arm A — Baseline control**  
Current irrigation and operating practice is instrumented but not optimized.

**Arm B — RootIQ Precision Water**  
Root-zone sensing, weather / ET inputs and irrigation decision support are tested without AWG contribution.

**Arm C — Integrated water-secure farm**  
RootIQ plus the Aridon Water Independence System, storage and the AWG-1000 research platform. Energy-water scheduling can be added when the site supports it.

Optional subtests may include:

- livestock water + heat sentinel;
- agricultural early warning;
- Soil Water Battery materials research;
- nutrient-independence sensing / application workflows;
- energy-water microgrid scheduling.

## 5. Technology package

### Aridon Water Independence System

A control and decision layer for wells, atmospheric water generation, rain capture, recycled or brackish sources, storage, treatment, water quality, weather and crop demand.

Primary R&D questions:

- How much conventional water withdrawal can integrated source management avoid?
- What operating rules best balance resilience, energy cost and water cost?
- Can a useful Water Security Days metric predict farm operating resilience?

### AWG-1000 Southwest research platform

A containerized atmospheric-water research platform intended to test hybrid operating strategies in hot, low-humidity climates.

Candidate modes include:

- direct condensation when dew-point conditions are favorable;
- desiccant moisture capture during lower-relative-humidity periods;
- thermal regeneration using solar heat, waste heat or another verified source;
- automated mode scheduling using dew point, humidity, temperature, energy price and storage state.

**Important:** Aridon’s existing engineering package treats approximately 1,000 liters/day as a nominal design objective under an engineer-defined reference climate, not a guaranteed output under all conditions.

### RootIQ Precision Water

Root-zone moisture / tension sensors at multiple depths combined with weather, crop stage and irrigation history.

Primary R&D question: **Can the farm reduce water and pumping energy without reducing marketable production or profit?**

### Farm Profit Digital Twin

A software model connecting crop or livestock performance to water, energy, labor, financing, equipment and input costs.

Primary output: **profit per acre / animal / operating unit**, with uncertainty and assumptions visible.

### Aridon Farm R&D Network

A common process for matching researchers and technologies with willing field sites while preserving farmer permissions, data provenance and standardized trial outcomes.

## 6. Equipment and instrumentation requirements

Final equipment must be site-designed and engineering-reviewed. The demonstration planning basis includes:

### Water-production / treatment equipment

- one engineered and instrumented AWG-1000 research prototype;
- site-sized raw / product water storage;
- sanitary condensate collection and first-flush provisions;
- water-treatment skid and UV / disinfection as required by intended use;
- pumps, valves, pressure controls and sampling points;
- temperature, conductivity and treatment-status sensing.

### Measurement equipment

- calibrated product-water flow meter;
- dedicated electrical energy meter;
- weather station measuring temperature, humidity, dew point and relevant weather variables;
- tank-level instrumentation;
- root-zone moisture / tension sensors at multiple depths;
- irrigation flow or runtime measurement;
- pump energy measurement where practical;
- telemetry gateway and timestamped data logger;
- maintenance, alarm and consumables event logging.

### Laboratory / verification

- baseline source-water testing as applicable;
- product-water laboratory testing under the approved intended-use protocol;
- calibration records;
- data-quality and missing-data flags;
- independent protocol review and final technical memorandum.

## 9. 12-month work plan

### Gate 1 — Weeks 0–6: co-design and protocol

- select the primary field-validation site;
- define intended use of produced water;
- recruit producer cohort;
- execute data-rights and confidentiality framework;
- identify independent verifier;
- establish reference climate and success criteria.

### Gate 2 — Months 1–3: engineering and baseline

- complete paid feasibility / DFM phase;
- begin 90-day producer cohort;
- establish water, energy, root-zone, economic and operating baselines;
- finalize site and instrumentation design.

### Gate 3 — Months 3–5: fabrication and site readiness

- fabricate prototype;
- prepare storage, treatment and electrical interfaces;
- install field sensors;
- complete factory acceptance testing.

### Gate 4 — Month 6: commissioning

- complete site acceptance testing;
- establish approved intended-use water-quality release process;
- train operators;
- lock baseline and measurement protocol.

### Gate 5 — Months 6–11: 180-day field operation

- collect timestamped water, energy, weather, irrigation and reliability data;
- perform scheduled laboratory sampling;
- record maintenance and corrective actions;
- compare trial arms;
- preserve raw evidence and data-quality flags.

### Gate 6 — Month 12: independent review and scale decision

Deliver:

- independent technical memorandum;
- farmer usefulness / economics report;
- public-learning summary where authorized;
- policy / replication brief;
- go / redesign / stop recommendation;
- next-site replication package.

## 10. Success metrics

### AWG / water system

- verified daily and seasonal gallons / liters produced;
- kWh per liter / gallon;
- output by temperature / humidity / dew-point band;
- water-quality laboratory results;
- uptime;
- faults and maintenance events;
- consumables and O&M labor;
- cost per usable gallon under the specific site conditions.

### Precision-water / agronomic system

- gallons per acre or water per unit of production;
- irrigation events and duration;
- pump kWh;
- root-zone stress hours;
- yield / marketable production where applicable;
- nutrient and runoff indicators where included;
- profit per acre / production unit.

### Producer and software outcomes

- engagement;
- administrative time saved;
- margin / cost opportunities identified;
- action taken on recommendations;
- satisfaction and trust;
- willingness to continue / host replication.

### Early-warning outcomes, where tested

- warning lead time;
- false-alert rate;
- producer action rate;
- avoided loss or disruption where evidence supports the conclusion.

## 11. Evidence and data governance

The pilot should operate under five non-negotiable evidence rules:

1. **Measured and modeled results are always separated.**
2. **Farmer operating data remain permissioned.**
3. **Independent reviewers can inspect the agreed evidence trail.**
4. **Missing data, sensor faults and corrections are preserved rather than hidden.**
5. **Public claims use only results supported by the reviewed dataset.**

Public summaries should default to aggregate or anonymized producer data unless the producer explicitly approves attribution.
