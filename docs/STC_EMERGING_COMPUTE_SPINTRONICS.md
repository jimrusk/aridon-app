# STC Emerging Compute Technology Program

## Antiferromagnetic Spintronics

Add antiferromagnetic spintronics to the Southwest Technology Campus (STC) advanced-computing technology roadmap.

### Research basis
A 2026 Science paper reported picosecond ultralow-power switching using an antiferromagnetic Mn3Sn-based device. This is an emerging R&D technology, not a demonstrated 1,000x faster production computer.

### STC objective
Position STC as a real-world evaluation and demonstration site for emerging low-power compute, memory, interconnect and AI infrastructure technologies rather than locking the campus exclusively to current GPU architectures.

### Technology tracks
- Antiferromagnetic spintronic switching and nonvolatile memory
- Photonic/electrical high-speed interconnects
- Quantum and hybrid computing
- Next-generation AI accelerators
- Low-power server architectures
- Advanced cooling and heat-recovery systems

### Campus evaluation loop
Emerging hardware -> benchmark compute performance -> measure electrical consumption -> measure heat output -> measure cooling energy/water -> calculate total cost of ownership -> test workload suitability -> produce independent deployment evidence.

### STC infrastructure implication
Design future compute/test areas with modular power, cooling and instrumentation so new accelerator/memory systems can be tested without rebuilding the facility. Capture rack-level power, thermal load, cooling-water use, compute throughput, reliability and cost per workload.

### Strategic connection
If antiferromagnetic spintronics reaches commercial deployment, its value to STC should be evaluated through the full infrastructure chain:
ultralow-power switching -> potential lower compute energy -> potential lower heat load -> potential lower cooling demand -> potential lower water/energy consumption -> improved compute economics.

Do not claim those downstream benefits until demonstrated at system level.

### Partnership targets
Research universities, national laboratories, semiconductor/device manufacturers, AI hardware companies, photonics companies and advanced-computing programs can use STC as a deployment/test environment when the technology reaches suitable readiness.
